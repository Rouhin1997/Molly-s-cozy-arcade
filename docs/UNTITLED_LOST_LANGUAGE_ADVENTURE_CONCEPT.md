# Untitled Lost-Language Adventure — commercial game concept & market review

**Checkpoint:** 14 November 2026  
**Project type:** Standalone, original, commercial-intent single-player narrative adventure, **not** a Molly's Cozy Arcade game  
**Working title:** *Untitled Lost-Language Adventure* (a descriptive placeholder only; no final title selected or cleared)  
**Status:** CONCEPT RETAINED / PREPRODUCTION CANDIDATE. No game or mechanics implemented.

## 1. Founder's original premise (preserve this)

In a world once connected by language, a divine entity is wounded and angered by humanity's destructive use of words — hatred, cruelty, lies, and the conflicts they enable. The entity curses mankind by taking language away.

Over decades, systems that depend on shared meaning disintegrate: government, coordination, commerce, repairs, mutual aid, treaties and peace negotiations. Wars, fear and isolation follow. Survivors withdraw into scattered communities, wary of other human beings.

After witnessing the suffering, the entity takes pity and offers a **path to redemption** — not by instantly restoring language, but by allowing humanity to **rediscover and rebuild communication itself**.

A **young shepherd and his dog** travel through the fragmented world, uncover language, help survivors communicate and gradually rebuild communities and civilization.

**Non-negotiable narrative priority:** a genuine emotional roller coaster, mixing wonder, tenderness, grief, tension and difficult choices. When the protagonists encounter meanings such as hatred, cruelty, betrayal, accusations or other hurtful expressions, players should feel a sincere dilemma about *whether, when and how* those meanings should be shared. Their consequences must be visible in the world.

**Important nuance:** The curse removes a means of expressing and sharing meaning; it does **not** remove humanity's emotions. Rediscovering a term like *hatred* does not create hatred. Naming it can enable manipulation or aggression, **but also recognition, accountability, consent and healing**. Avoid portraying permanent suppression of difficult concepts as a trivially correct solution.

## 2. Worldbuilding problems to solve before implementation

- Why has language not simply re-emerged through gestures, drawings, signs or new conventional symbols over decades? Candidate solution: the curse temporarily blocks the capacity to build **stable, shared symbolic meaning**, rather than merely erasing vocabulary. The entity's reprieve relaxes that restriction. This mechanism is **proposed, not canon**.
- How can people still cooperate instinctively, recognize physical danger, and form bonds without invalidating the curse? Define the boundary between ordinary perception and shared abstract expression.
- What traces of old civilization remain (ruins, objects, monuments, rituals) if inhabitants cannot interpret written or spoken language? Decide whether traces are meaningful clues, misleading artifacts or both.
- How does the shepherd acquire the first meaningful symbols? **Possible hybrid model:** encounter ancient traces, observe lived experience, and co-create a new communication system with NPCs; do not simply restore an English dictionary.
- What can the dog uniquely understand? Wordless gestures, scent, behaviour and trust; make the dog a believable autonomous companion and essential source of early discovery rather than a generic puzzle switch.
- What limits the divine entity's interference? It supplies an opportunity, **not** solutions, instructions or a simplistic morality test.

## 3. Gameplay pillars to prototype

1. **Observe and infer meaning:** Interpret actions, relationships and environmental context instead of choosing an unexplained glyph-to-English matching answer.
2. **Build shared expressions:** Assemble discovered ideas/relationships into a new communicative act; model meaning internally in language-independent semantic data so localization is possible.
3. **Try communication with consequence:** Attempt expressions with NPCs; recognize ambiguity or misunderstanding from natural reactions and later outcomes. Avoid a huge unrestricted language generator initially.
4. **Companion-driven cooperation:** Shepherd and dog communicate effectively nonverbally, serving as an emotional and mechanical reference for human reconstruction.
5. **Witness social transformation:** New communication opens cooperation, repairs infrastructure, restores exchanges, resolves or intensifies disputes. Show before/after changes in art, sound, traffic and social behaviour.
6. **Earn moral complexity:** Harmful concepts present timing/context/trust decisions, not a universal virtue meter or a collection of evil words. The player should sometimes regret actions without being irreversibly punished for experimentation.

**Illustrative short quest:** A settlement's irrigation and waterwheel have broken down. The shepherd and dog help interpret needs, learn how to communicate *water*, *danger*, *help* and *together*, and coordinate a repair. An accusation introduces *blame*; choosing how to teach the community to discuss responsibility affects relationships. The first mutually understood exchange is staged as an intimate emotional moment rather than a bright "vocabulary unlocked" banner.

**First meaningful dilemma:** An accusation may help an injured person be understood or inflame a dispute. Withholding it might delay harm but also obstruct justice. A third approach could teach evidence/responsibility alongside blame; even that raises questions about who controls language. These are **narrative options**, not predetermined moral rewards.

## 4. Art and world scope — current preferred approach

**Tentative style:** **Cinematic hand-painted 2.5D / illustrated 2D**, with expressive foreground characters, parallax landscapes, a camera that alternates between overwhelming ruined vistas and intimate gestures, and carefully controlled colour transformations as settlements reconnect. **Art style is not locked**; test on a playable scene before committing to expensive assets.

Do **not** build a third-person MMO, photorealistic 3D open world or procedural country-scale map for the first release. Scale comes from strategically framed wide backgrounds, a handful of explorable interconnected locations, strong environmental storytelling and revisiting the same scenes after they visibly change.

Narrative mood changes: oppressive silence -> surprising connection -> hope -> misuse/misunderstanding -> conflict -> deliberate reconciliation. Audio restoration should be just as deliberate as visual restoration.

### Possible commercial first game
- A complete authored region/valley containing several isolated settlements instead of a planetary open world.
- Tentative finished-game target: about **3–5 hours** (far from a commitment; may still require years of part-time production).
- A self-contained story and finale that work without a sequel.

## 5. Solo/small-team production constraints and workflow

**Provisional engine:** [Godot](https://godotengine.org/license/) 4 with GDScript; free/open-source MIT engine supports commercial releases, subject to applicable distribution notices. Create a **separate game repository** once work begins; do not mix production code with the arcade.

**Other possible tools:** Krita for layered illustrations; Godot 2D animations or rigged sprites; Audacity for effects; Git/GitHub for versioned development. Start with Windows PC, not simultaneous PC/mobile/console releases. Use original or properly licensed commercial assets; track rights for generated and third-party artwork, sounds, fonts and music.

**Do not spend early money on a large soundtrack, expensive asset commissions or storefront launch.** Test the central meaning-building loop before high-fidelity production.

### Milestone sequence
1. **Mechanics sketch (greybox):** shepherd movement, autonomous dog following, one NPC, 3–5 concepts, first shared expression, one visible consequence. No high-production artwork required.
2. **Emotional vertical slice:** one settlement, a 15–30-minute authored story, waterwheel/irrigation problem, interaction with dog, a carefully scoped difficult communication decision, world-state before/after.
3. **Blind external playtest:** testers unfamiliar with the premise should understand what language discovery means without extensive tutorial or a designer explaining it. Note confusion, emotional response and time-to-solve.
4. **Public demo decision:** expand/polish the opening to ~30–60 minutes only after validation. Test wishlists/feedback and presentation.
5. **Full product go/no-go:** budget, realistic schedule, art workload, accessibility/localization, QA and marketing; choose publisher/funding/self-release then.

**Prototype pass conditions:** language discovery is actually fun; the dog matters; the player understands expression construction; mistakes produce meaningful and fair feedback; decisions alter the world; art communicates a convincing sense of scale.

### Research conclusion and positioning

- **The broad theme is clearly occupied:** language barriers, symbolic decoding, cultures separated by distrust, and Babel motifs have established competitors.
- **The full combination appears unclaimed among the listings reviewed**, but there could be smaller, unannounced, non-English, mobile or unindexed works that this scan did not find.
- **Market signal:** Existing successful language-focused indie adventures make the niche commercially plausible; Steam review counts and genre interest are not revenue projections or proof that this particular project will sell.
- **Differentiation to protect:** the player is restoring the *capacity* for shared language, **not translating five existing dialects**; their acts of communication visibly rebuild society, and the moral question is **how to transmit dangerous meanings responsibly**.
- **Mechanic risk:** our early proposal to concatenate simple concepts (e.g., WATER + DANGER) risks looking like other word-combination games unless it develops a distinctive in-world representation and meaningful NPC interpretation.
- **Avoid marketing as "a more emotional Chants of Sennaar"** or any reference-driven clone. Market it through its own premise, aesthetic, protagonist/dog bond, evolving social world and complex consequences.

**Working one-line positioning:** *In a world where humanity has forgotten how to share meaning, a young shepherd and his dog discover language — and learn that giving people their voices back can heal, divide or change them forever.*

## 7. Next design questions (do not silently treat proposals as locked)

1. **What precisely did the divine curse remove?** Distinguish spoken language, signing, writing and symbolic communication; establish an internally logical rule.
2. **What does the first successful communication look like on screen?** Discover a playable expressive mechanic rather than an ordinary matching quiz.
3. **How can the companion dog help the protagonist infer meaning through a natural bond?** Avoid reducing the companion to a puzzle tool.
4. **How does an NPC interpret the player's expression?** Can ambiguous phrases be evaluated fairly without combinatorial explosion?
5. **How do villagers rebuild?** Define minimal, meaningful scene state changes across revisits.
6. **Which difficult concepts cause the first emotionally credible dilemma?** Design consequences grounded in people, not an arbitrary good/evil meter.
7. **Which art direction is feasible at our actual skill, time and asset budget?** Hand-painted 2.5D is a candidate, not a final production lock.
8. **Can the first 20 minutes be enjoyable without an exposition prologue or dependency on English?**
